Recommendation: retain the bounded compatibility spike first. Propose three policies for the subsequent coding diagnostic: frozen live ADRL, fixed frontier, and fixed cheapest permitted available Claude deployment. Apply identical safety restrictions and explicit fallback/transition rules to all policies. Record all actual served deployments. A forced cheap spike does not demonstrate merit-based selection. Do not silently repair or reconfigure the router based on evaluation cases.

The third arm exposes cases where a cheaper model succeeded despite ADRL choosing frontier. Describe this as observed excess cost relative to successful tested alternatives, not the cheapest viable model globally or established statistical regret. One attempt per arm cannot establish a model's success probability. Identical ADRL/frontier choices provide little selection contrast, although independent executions can reveal variability or proxy overhead. Preserve them if the frozen design specifies independent runs; do not reuse outcomes silently.

Budget correction: six executable coding classes times three arms is 18 model task-arm attempts; the restricted/private class is a separate three-policy enforcement control expecting zero disallowed upstream requests. Use synthetic restricted input, not actual secrets. Thus seven classes produce 21 matrix cells, not 21 expected billable model runs. No permitted destination means block for all arms, including fixed frontier. A permitted-but-unavailable local destination must not silently fall back to cloud. Controls never enter savings or capability labels. Nine planning model attempts would be a later distinct budget. Existing ten-attempt limit remains until an explicit revised packet/budget disposition; no experiment launched here.

Do not assume test-addition routes cheap. The inspected synthetic lab previously routed test-work frontier. Run and retain the dry-check distribution under the frozen policy, with available Claude deployments. If fewer than two classes choose cheap, record failed contrast and reconsider before spending; any revised policy gets separate held-out tasks.

Economics: primary modeled economic metric is versioned rate-weighted complete task cost with mutually exclusive input/cache/output categories, all retries, and ADRL overhead. Report actual metered charges separately where available. Neither modeled cost nor raw tokens establishes reduced fixed-subscription charges or quota consumption. Publish all quality/cost results, including losses, even when the feasibility continuation bar passes. Pin rates and units before execution. Do not claim blanket negligible expense or account entitlement without measurement.

Proposed continuation bar: ADRL actually chooses cheaper on at least two executable classes and at least one pair passes the same mandatory criteria without critical defects at lower weighted cost than frontier. This permits a larger experiment only. Count frontier choices for which fixed-cheap also passed, and cheaper choices that failed while frontier passed. Missing evidence is inconclusive; zero successful cheaper cases, no routing contrast or failed control trigger reconsideration. No extrapolated population ROI from one task per class. Initial-choice comparison should hold transitions fixed; live escalation is a separately declared trajectory policy with all handoff costs, not an accidental extra variable.

Do not publish Jira tickets as part of artifact generation. Require estimates only when justified by source information; unknowns and assumptions are valid outputs. Freeze rubric, answer key, shuffle seed and thresholds before outputs. Judge methodology can know the seed construction/severity distribution; hide exact answer keys and model identities from the performing models and threshold selection from their results. Seeded findings are not exhaustive truth: independently adjudicate valid unseeded findings rather than automatically calling them false alarms.
