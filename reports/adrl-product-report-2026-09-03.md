# ADRL as a product: a harness-side routing and privacy layer for the company engineering teams

Technical report, 2026-09-03. Companion to the adversarial critique in `reports/adrl-adversarial-critique-2026-09-03.md`. Register: this repository (77 decisions, nine buckets). Implementation: `~/projects/adrl-core`.

Evidence tags follow the research files: `fetched` means the cited page was retrieved and read on 2026-09-03; `search-only` means the claim rests on a search snippet that was not opened.

## 1. Executive summary

ADRL (Adaptive Routing Layer) is a transparent control layer that sits between a coding harness and the model gateway. The harness (Claude Code today, others by profile) points its base URL at ADRL instead of at the gateway. ADRL classifies every request, runs hard gates on it, decides once per user turn which capability rung should serve the turn (a local model on the developer's machine, a cheap cloud model, or a frontier model), records an immutable decision, and forwards the request to the gateway, which owns the concrete endpoint, provider, retry and transport. Continuations inherit the route to protect prompt caches. Deterministic trip-wires escalate at safe action boundaries. Every request that leaves the machine is recorded in a hash-chained egress ledger before it is sent. The layer collects the evidence a learned router would need and refuses to authorise one until the evidence gates pass.

Three things a gateway cannot do, and ADRL does:

- **The local decision.** No enterprise gateway or vendor auto-router has a local rung as a target. LiteLLM's strategies do not condition on prompt content or task difficulty; Bedrock routes between two models of one family; Copilot, Cursor, Kiro and OpenRouter route cloud-to-cloud and mostly hide the served model (section 2). ADRL sees the request before it leaves the laptop, so it can keep it there.
- **Gating on tool results with a durable pin.** Secrets in coding-agent traffic arrive in tool results (file reads, command output, fixtures), not in the developer's typed instruction. ADRL scans the new content of every request class, pins the session lineage local-only on a high-confidence finding, persists the pin before forwarding, inherits it to subagents, and releases it only through an audited, reason-coded human action (SAF-001, SAF-002, SAF-003).
- **An auditable answer to "did this code ever leave the machine?"** A write-ahead, content-free, hash-chained egress ledger with signed, off-device checkpoints records every destination by deployment, trust zone and geography, with the gateway's receipt where one exists (TRU-002, TRU-003). Under the 2026 zero-data-retention regime, where the vendor now hands verification to the customer, this is the record a payments company needs.

Who it is for: platform teams that run the model gateway (LiteLLM), security teams that own data classification and residency, and engineering teams that want local or cheaper models on routine work without a quality drop or a privacy exposure.

Honest status. The implementation is a D1/D2 build: about 21,000 source lines, 428 tests, static and adversarial suites green, but it has never run against organic traffic. Twenty-two register decisions were proposed on 2026-09-03 and await disposition. Per EVL-007 as drafted, a new implementation inherits no maturity above D2 from the earlier prototype. Nothing here is a production claim.

## 2. Positioning against the September 2026 market

### 2.1 Static routers have plateaued; execution-grounded routing is where gains are

Across 21 routing methods and five benchmarks, prompt-only routers converge into a narrow band far below the oracle because they learn global model-quality averages rather than instance-specific signals; on RouterBench the top 15 routers differ by 0.23 points [The Routing Plateau, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07587, fetched]. A 400K-instance benchmark found several recent approaches, commercial routers included, fail to reliably beat a simple baseline [LLMRouterBench, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07206, fetched]. The oracle itself is overstated: with selection-valid intervals, the strongest deployable prompt router recovers only 7.5 to 14.4 percent of a 9.7 to 30.7 point oracle gap [Opportunity Is Not Realizability, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08265, fetched].

The gains come from conditioning on execution. SWE-Router lets a cheap model run exploratory turns and routes on the partial trajectory [SWE-Router, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00053, fetched]. Agent-as-a-Router frames routing as a context, action and feedback loop with a verifier and memory [Agent-as-a-Router, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22902, fetched]. MTRouter learns turn-level utility from logged trajectories and cuts cost 43 to 59 percent while making fewer switches [MTRouter, ACL 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23530, fetched]. TRACE-Router argues that not switching mid-workflow is what makes credit assignment possible [TRACE-Router, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22465, fetched]. Scrouting's ablation is the sharpest caution: a 7B scout producing a verified handoff let the cheapest fixer alone match the best single model on SWE-bench Pro Python at a fifth of the cost, so the handoff rather than the routing decision carried the result [Scrouting, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04804, fetched].

What this means for ADRL: the register's tenets (route turns not requests, sticky continuations, escalate at boundaries, learn only from verified outcomes) are the shape the 2026 results converge on. The initial per-turn decision (RTG-002) is the plateau by construction unless a trajectory-conditioned decision at each safe boundary is added, which is exactly the RTG-007 amendment proposed on 2026-09-03. The critique report covers this in detail.

### 2.2 Vendor auto-routers are cloud-to-cloud and opaque

Copilot's auto mode weighs availability, reasoning, code-generation complexity, bug-diagnosis difficulty and tool-orchestration needs, routes along natural cache boundaries because mid-session switching showed increased cost without ample improvements in quality, gives a 10 percent discount, and offers policy exclusions for residency but no local or on-premises target [About Copilot auto model selection, 2026, https://docs.github.com/copilot/concepts/auto-model-selection, fetched]. Cursor Router (July 22, 2026) exposes Intelligence, Balance and Cost dials and hides the routed model by default [Cursor Router changelog, 2026, https://cursor.com/changelog/router, fetched]. Kiro's Auto is a 1.0x credit baseline over a pool from GPT-5.6 to Qwen3 Coder Next and users file issues asking which model was used [Kiro Models docs, 2026, https://kiro.dev/docs/models/, fetched]. OpenRouter's Auto classifies into task types and ranks models by community spend over a trailing seven-day window [OpenRouter model routing, 2026, https://openrouter.ai/blog/insights/model-routing/, fetched]. Not Diamond Code routes per turn on session state and the state of the KV cache and may stay on an expensive model to keep a warm cache [Not Diamond Code, 2026, https://www.notdiamond.ai/blog/not-diamond-code-intelligent-model-routing-for-coding-agents, fetched]. vLLM's session-aware router hard-locks switches during tool loops and reports a 79 percent switch reduction across 21,600 turns [vLLM SAAR, 2026, https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-06-02-session-aware-agentic-routing, fetched].

Every one of these routes at cache boundaries, which vindicates FND-003 and SEM-003. None targets a local rung, none learns from an organisation's own verified outcomes, and most do not tell the customer which model served the request. That last point matters for audit: OPS-006 requires the served identity to be recorded, and TRU-002 requires it at deployment granularity.

### 2.3 Enterprise gateways route on infrastructure metadata

LiteLLM routes on simple-shuffle, least-busy, usage, latency and cost, with context-window and region pre-call filters and content-policy fallbacks; no strategy conditions on prompt content or task difficulty [LiteLLM routing docs, 2026, https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/routing, fetched]. Portkey's conditional routing evaluates metadata, request parameters and URL path only [Portkey conditional routing, 2026, https://portkey.ai/docs/product/ai-gateway/conditional-routing, fetched]. Kong's AI Proxy Advanced adds semantic and consistent-hash balancing, enterprise-only [Kong AI Proxy Advanced, 2026, https://developer.konghq.com/plugins/ai-proxy-advanced/, fetched]. Cloudflare AI Gateway is edge-only and documented as incompatible with Regional Services [Kosmoy, 2026, https://www.kosmoy.com/resources/blog/cloudflare-ai-gateway-alternatives/, fetched]. Bedrock intelligent prompt routing picks between exactly two models of one family, is English-only, and cannot adjust routing decisions based on application-specific performance data [AWS Bedrock docs, 2026, https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/prompt-routing.html, fetched]. Azure's model router routes across five vendors with Quality, Balanced and Cost modes and honours data zones, but its context window is capped by the smallest pooled model, routing is text-only, and cache benefit exists only when the same model handles consecutive requests [Microsoft Learn model router, updated 2026-09-01, https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/openai/concepts/model-router, fetched].

ADRL is not a competitor to the gateway. FND-002 makes the split explicit: ADRL owns semantic policy (which rung, which deployment set, what is forbidden), the gateway owns mechanical execution (endpoint, provider, retry, transport, health). The gateway is where the company's LiteLLM investment stays; ADRL adds what the gateway cannot see.

### 2.4 Zero data retention became verify-it-yourself

Anthropic's ZDR for Claude Code is per-organisation, requires separate enablement, excludes third-party MCP data, disables cloud sessions and artifacts, and Fable 5.x models require data retention by default under the Covered Models policy [Zero data retention, Claude Code docs, 2026, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/zero-data-retention, fetched]. Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (September 2, 2026) moves safety monitoring onto customer-controlled cloud and sends signals to customers to review, so the verification burden shifts to the enterprise [The Register, 2026-09-02, https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/02/anthropic-promises-zero-data-retention-but-customers-must-check-it-worked/5293789, fetched]. OpenAI announced ZDR for frontier models with Private Safety Processing on August 21, 2026 [OpenAI, 2026, https://openai.com/index/offering-zero-data-retention-for-frontier-models/, search-only]. US-only inference carries a 1.1x multiplier on the Claude API [Anthropic pricing docs, 2026, https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing, fetched]. Under the EU AI Act, GPAI provider obligations and Article 50 transparency took effect August 2, 2026; the Digital Omnibus pushed Annex III high-risk duties to December 2, 2027 [SIG EU AI Act summary, 2026, https://www.softwareimprovementgroup.com/blog/eu-ai-act-summary/, fetched].

When the vendor's promise is "we do not retain, and you should check", the customer needs its own record of what was sent, to whom, and under what verdict. That is the egress ledger (TRU-003) and the reason SAF-008's data-class ceiling was promoted into a trust bucket.

### 2.5 Cost dynamics favour cache-aware routing

Cache reads are 0.1x base input on most Claude models and 0.025x on Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1; writes are 1.25x (5 minute) and 2x (1 hour); batch is 50 percent off and stacks with caching [Anthropic pricing docs, 2026, fetched]. OpenAI cached input is about 10 percent of list and default cache retention moved to 24 hours on May 29, 2026 [Morph OpenAI pricing, 2026, search-only]. A switch pays a cache write on the new model and forfeits reads on the old one. RTG-009 makes this the cost unit routing optimises: session-marginal, after cache effects, never list price alone.

## 3. Architecture

### 3.1 The nine buckets

The register organises 77 decisions into nine buckets. Flow: SEM, SAF, RTG, CAS, MEM, then LRN and EVL; OPS supports every stage; FND defines the boundary. Nine tenets bind all of them (route turns not requests; safety precedes optimisation; local-first conditionally; rules own clear cases; escalation is mechanical and boundary-safe; state is sticky within an episode; capability choice is separate from endpoint choice; evidence is cause-clean; learning cannot self-promote).

**FND, System Boundary and Principles (five decisions, D2 to D3).** Who owns what. FND-001 defines transparency at the wire level: the frontier path forwards the original bytes and relays response bytes untouched, with the one documented carve-out for thinking suppression after an escalation. FND-002 splits semantic policy (ADRL) from mechanical execution (gateway) and, as amended on 2026-09-03, makes the permitted deployment set the monotone object the gateway must honour. FND-003 fixes the routing boundary at the user turn, mechanically defined, with gates on every request. FND-004 (replaced on 2026-09-02) fails open only for unpinned lineages and fails closed for pinned ones, recording every fail-open by class. FND-005 says scope expands only through measured gates; component presence is not readiness.

**SEM, Interaction Semantics (seven, D0 to D3).** What a request means. SEM-001 classifies six request classes mechanically from path, body shape and the `x-claude-code-*` headers: user turn, continuation, utility, subagent, pre-warm, passthrough, each with a `content_bearing` flag. SEM-002 derives the session key (header first, then `metadata.user_id`, then a per-connection fallback) and composes it with agent lineage; only an HMAC is stored. SEM-003 (approved, recommended D3) makes continuations inherit the sticky route. SEM-004 splits utility calls into cosmetic and context-bearing. SEM-005 enumerates episode-boundary signals that release only the escalation ratchet, never a pin. SEM-006 makes subagents inherit constraints now and routing later. SEM-007 (proposed) defines protocol profiles: Anthropic Messages is profile v1, and the OpenAI Responses API needs its own adapter, discriminator and corpus before Codex enters scope.

**SAF, Safety, Privacy, Hard Constraints (nine, D1 to D3).** What is forbidden or infeasible. SAF-001 runs hard gates on every request class in order: data-class ceiling, new-content secret scan, feasibility; outcomes only tighten within a lineage; a fallback path can never override a gate. SAF-002 is the one-way pin: durable across restart, lineage-scoped, covering count_tokens, compaction and forks, released only by an audited human action with a reason code. SAF-003 scans new content per request with detectors tiered by measured precision and retroactive suppression of derived artefacts. SAF-004 and SAF-005 define how a pinned lineage fails loudly in the harness's dialect and how a block stays recoverable (the harness auto-compacts and retries). SAF-006 removes infeasible rungs before optimisation. SAF-007 (replaced) runs verification in an OS-enforced sandbox with no egress. SAF-008 and SAF-009 were proposed on 2026-09-02 and are marked superseded by the TRU bucket pending disposition; SAF-008's PII tier stays in SAF.

**TRU, Trust, Residency and Egress (three, proposed D0).** Whom the content may be sent to and how that is proven. TRU-001: repository identity comes from a launcher-signed assertion tied to SCM inventory and a content fingerprint, never from prompt text; unknown identity is local-only. TRU-002: the permitted set is a signed endpoint inventory entry (deployment id, rung, provider, api_base, trust zone, geo, data-use profile); a local deployment must be loopback and local_host; dispatch yields a served-deployment receipt. TRU-003: automatic signed checkpoints under a separate key, off-device anchoring with acknowledgements, and a receipt-based answer to whether content left the machine.

**RTG, Routing Intelligence and Economics (nine, D0 to D3).** Which permitted rung is the best choice. RTG-001 defines three rungs (local, cheap_cloud, frontier) by measured boundaries, amended to be economic tiers with approved escalation edges rather than a total capability order. RTG-002 selects the cheapest healthy rung whose named estimator clears a versioned threshold. RTG-003 lets deterministic rules own clear cases with measured, revocable precision; the ambiguous band is a dated measurement. RTG-004 permits local-first only when a bounded, clean cascade is feasible; pinned sessions are local-or-block, never local-first. RTG-005 states the objective (verified quality, retry risk, latency, session-marginal cost). RTG-006 bounds an advisory LLM classifier to the ambiguous band, local or skipped on pinned sessions. RTG-007 sets the learned target with a pre-build gate and, as amended, a temporal continue-versus-escalate estimand at each safe boundary. RTG-008 separates rung from endpoint with a gateway leak contract. RTG-009 (proposed) is the cache-aware cost unit.

**CAS, Execution, Cascade, Recovery (nine, D0 to D3).** How the choice is executed, retried or escalated. CAS-001 defines deterministic trip-wires (repeated calls, dialect-invalid calls, tool-error repetition, attempt-budget exhaustion, verifier failure) with per-rung versioned thresholds and a published miss rate. CAS-002 fixes the seven-type `failure-types-v2` enum and its precedence rule. CAS-003 defines the action boundary (every tool_use id answered) and forbids re-issue after streamed tool content; amended to accept MCP hints only from allow-listed servers and to record read-only calls. CAS-004 replaces one stripping rule with a versioned per-provider-pair table for reasoning blocks and tool ids. CAS-005 makes escalation sticky within an episode. CAS-006 records served model and provider with provenance. CAS-007 gives ADRL zero retries and bounds the gateway's. CAS-008 (proposed) scopes escalation under subagents. CAS-009 (proposed) is the action-effect provenance and authorisation boundary.

**MEM, Memory, Evidence, Label Integrity (ten, D1 to D3).** What happened and whether the record can be trusted. MEM-001 is the strictly append-only ledger keyed by route_id with schema versions, idempotency keys and a logged-erasure clause. MEM-002 is the three-state outcome lifecycle (pending, closed_turn, closed_final) with a versioned closing rule and censoring of non-final labels. MEM-003 makes verification an appended event with provenance, tree identity and an indeterminate result. MEM-004 types labels on `failure-types-v2` with a safe default of unverifiable. MEM-005 (replaced) classes embeddings and hashes as prompt-class data with retroactive shredding on pin. MEM-006 is the fail-safe facade that is never silent. MEM-007 makes projections rebuildable with identity stamps and as-of rebuilds. MEM-008 keeps retrieval advisory until gated. MEM-009 binds counterfactuals to explicit route_ids. MEM-010 (proposed) is retention and erasure by crypto-shredding with a field-level data inventory.

**LRN, Learning and Adaptation (eight, D0 to D2).** How a better policy is learned. LRN-001 defines evidence tiers with a T1-only objective. LRN-002 requires branched pairs as live branches with a declared power target. LRN-003 names the estimand as a conditional average treatment effect with two pairwise effects for three rungs. LRN-004 enforces pre-decision features by construction (decision-time snapshot, as-of projections, temporal splits, deny-list). LRN-005 versions every artifact. LRN-006 requires abstention with a risk-coverage target before any estimator. LRN-007 forbids autonomous promotion. LRN-008 (proposed) is bounded, propensity-logged exploration in the ambiguous band.

**EVL, Evaluation, Graduation, Rollout (nine, proposed D0 to D1).** Whether behaviour is ready for more exposure. First captured on 2026-09-03: baseline set (EVL-001), holdout and calibration protocol (EVL-002), branch protocol and replay prohibition (EVL-003), label quantity and quality gate (EVL-004), synthetic is not organic (EVL-005), offline evaluation report (EVL-006), human graduation and the D2 to D5 ladder including the rule that a new implementation inherits no maturity above D2 (EVL-007), scorecard format (EVL-008), and blockers never averaged away (EVL-009).

**OPS, Platform, Runtime, Operations (eight, proposed D0 to D2).** How the system is operated, observed and rolled back. First captured on 2026-09-03: single-process constraint and state inventory (OPS-001), key custody and rotation (OPS-002), signed endpoint inventory and change control (OPS-003), backup, restore and erasure (OPS-004), shadow-mode semantics with no authority (OPS-005), served identity at deployment level (OPS-006), audit-anchor availability and incident runbooks (OPS-007), and fail-open SLOs with bypass audit (OPS-008).

### 3.2 How a request flows

Every request follows one path. Stages are ports with one production adapter each, so shadow mode and fault injection swap adapters rather than branch. The bucket and decisions that govern each stage are given in brackets.

1. **Ingress.** The full body is read; a read-only JSON view is parsed; headers are captured; hop-by-hop headers are stripped and `Accept-Encoding: identity` is forced upstream. The original bytes are kept for the frontier path [FND-001].
2. **Classify.** Path, final block type, `max_tokens`, tool-list presence, utility fingerprints and the `x-claude-code-*` headers yield the request class, `content_bearing` and `interaction_mode`. Unclassifiable on a pinned lineage never defaults to a cloud-bound class [SEM-001, SEM-004].
3. **Identity.** Session key by preference order, HMAC at rest, lineage as session plus agent chain, parent links persisted so the chain survives restart. The per-lineage lock is taken here and held through the ledger write [SEM-002, SEM-006].
4. **Gates.** In order, each shrinking the permitted deployment set: workload identity and data-class ceiling (TRU-001, TRU-002, SAF-008's PII tier), new-content secret scan (SAF-003), feasibility by tokenizer estimate and gateway health (SAF-006). A pinning finding narrows to local before any I/O; the pin is written through to both ledgers before the request proceeds; a write failure blocks. Gate exception resolves per FND-004 by pin state and failure class. In observe mode findings are recorded as shadow findings with no authority [SAF-001, SAF-002, OPS-005].
5. **Egress write-ahead.** An egress row with the intended deployment, trust zone, geo and gate verdicts is appended before dispatch. Append failure on a pinned lineage is a gate failure; on an unpinned lineage it marks `ledger_degraded` [TRU-003].
6. **Route or inherit.** A user turn gets a fresh decision inside the permitted set: bands and the named estimator, cascade feasibility, the advisory classifier only in the ambiguous band, cost per RTG-009. A `route_id` is minted and the decision row is written through with the feature snapshot and every policy version. Every other class inherits the sticky route [FND-003, SEM-003, RTG-002 to RTG-006, RTG-009, LRN-004].
7. **Cascade plan.** Is this an action boundary; did the previous response fire a wire; is escalation sticky. Escalation applies the provider-pair transform and appends the handoff note after the tool results [CAS-001, CAS-003, CAS-004, CAS-005].
8. **Rewrite.** The frontier path forwards the original bytes and the harness's model name. Other rungs get the rewrite (strip `thinking`, `cache_control`, beta fields; set the deployment alias). After an escalation whose source turn had no thinking blocks, thinking is suppressed until the next user turn [FND-001, CAS-004].
9. **Dispatch.** Exactly one attempt through the gateway client. A pinned lineage whose chosen deployment is not `local_host` is blocked regardless of the rung label [CAS-007, TRU-002].
10. **Relay and observe.** Bytes stream back untouched while an observer records the first streamed `tool_use`, the served model and deployment from the gateway's receipt headers, usage including cache tokens, and the stop reason. Absent a receipt the served source is `assumed_intended` and the egress row says so [FND-001, CAS-006, OPS-006, RTG-009].
11. **Cascade observe and ledger events.** Trip-wires are evaluated on the observation; outcome events (`pending`, `closed_turn`) and served-receipt rows are appended write-behind; sticky state is updated through the state provider [CAS-001, CAS-006, MEM-001, MEM-002].

Invariants enforced across the path: the permitted set can only shrink within a lineage and no fallback can widen it; the frontier path is byte-exact save the documented carve-out; every request of every class is gated; routing happens once per user turn; ADRL never retries and never re-issues after streamed tool content; the egress row precedes the bytes leaving.

### 3.3 How evidence flows

1. **Decision row** (MEM-001, LRN-004): immutable `route_id`, permitted deployments, decided rung, estimator and policy versions, decision-time feature snapshot, propensity.
2. **Pending, then closed_turn** (MEM-002): appended by the cascade at dispatch and at the next boundary, carrying served identity and usage.
3. **Verification** (MEM-003, SAF-007): a job runs allow-listed commands inside an OS sandbox on a git worktree snapshot; the event carries verifier version, tree identity, and pass, fail or indeterminate; a failure also arms the verifier trip-wire for the next boundary.
4. **closed_final** (MEM-002): emitted by the versioned `close-v1` rule; late evidence after that appends a correction that supersedes by sequence number, never an edit.
5. **Label** (MEM-004, CAS-002): the cause type from `failure-types-v2` with precedence, confidence and provenance; only `task_capability` at `closed_final` is evidence about a rung.
6. **Tier** (LRN-001, EVL-005): T1 verified organic, T2 verified but censored, T3 proxy, T4 synthetic, T5 counterfactual pairs, plus `explore`; pooling across tiers is refused by construction.
7. **Pairs and exploration** (LRN-002, LRN-008, EVL-003): live branches from the same turn state, never replays; logged propensities in the ambiguous band only.
8. **Estimator and abstention** (LRN-003, LRN-006): a CATE with calibrated uncertainty; abstention with a declared risk target is built and tested first.
9. **Offline evaluation report and graduation** (EVL-006, EVL-007, LRN-007): a hashed report against the fixed baselines, then an explicit human signature on the artifact manifest; the router refuses any artifact without one.
10. **Retention and erasure** (MEM-005, MEM-010, OPS-004): prompt-class artefacts encrypted under per-session keys; erasure is key deletion plus an `erased` event; the evidence skeleton survives.

Across this flow the denominators travel with the numbers: excluded fractions (pinned, suppressed, degraded, subagent) are reported next to every metric (EVL-008), and pre-registered blockers are never averaged away (EVL-009).

## 4. Product surface area

### 4.1 Four integration depths

- **L0, passthrough with egress accounting.** ADRL relays without classification or routing and records every request as egress by destination. Useful on day one for any harness that can be repointed, and the only option for harnesses that cannot be classified.
- **L1, gated passthrough.** Requests are classified for `content_bearing`, the SAF gates run, pins are enforced, and the egress ledger records verdicts. No routing decision is made; the harness's requested model is honoured unless a pin forbids it.
- **L2, routed.** Full SEM classification, RTG decision per user turn, CAS cascade and escalation, evidence collected. Requires a protocol profile with identity on the wire or a per-developer credential mapping.
- **L3, routed with hook ingestion.** The harness's hooks feed tool results, tool failures, compaction and subagent lifecycle events into the ledger, closing the gaps wire-shape discrimination leaves (CAS-001 hook signals, SEM-005 boundary signals, CAS-008 subagent scope).

Three facts shape the surface. First, only Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode put a session and lineage identity on the model wire; every other harness offers at most a bearer key, so identity must come from hooks or per-developer credentials [survey, fetched]. Second, Codex speaks only the Responses API, and Copilot BYOK, OpenCode, Junie and Aider can also emit OpenAI formats, so the SEM-007 Responses profile is the gating item for anything beyond the Claude family [survey, fetched]. Third, a large class is un-gatewayable by design: Cursor routes all model traffic through its own servers, Kiro is AWS-signed with no BYOK, and Codex cloud, Copilot coding agent, Devin, Jules, Amp and Antigravity are vendor-hosted [survey, fetched].

### 4.3 Three the company topologies

**Developer laptop with a local model, ADRL and LiteLLM.** ADRL runs on the laptop next to a llama.cpp or MLX server; `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` points at ADRL; ADRL's gateway is the enterprise gateway. The local rung is a loopback deployment in the signed inventory (TRU-002, OPS-003). Pins keep pinned lineages on the laptop; everything else goes to the enterprise gateway with rung-closed fallback groups generated from `rungs.yaml` (FND-002). The launcher (`adrl launch --repo`) inventories the repository and injects the workload assertion (TRU-001). This is the L2 to L3 topology and the only one where the local rung exists.

**Shared team proxy.** ADRL runs as a service in front of the enterprise gateway for a team whose harnesses can be repointed but whose laptops cannot host a model. There is no local rung; the value is gating, pins, receipts and evidence at L1, plus routing between cheap_cloud and frontier at L2. OPS-001's single-process constraint applies today: one worker per deployment until state moves behind a multi-process provider.

**Un-gatewayable harnesses.** Cursor, Kiro, Antigravity, Jules, Devin, Amp, Codex cloud and the Copilot coding agent never send model traffic through ADRL. ADRL can consume their hooks where those exist (Cursor's enterprise `hooks.json` with `failClosed`, Kiro's `PreToolUse`) to run the secret scanner on tool results and record findings, and it records their traffic as unclassified egress in the ledger so the audit answer stays honest (TRU-003 scope clause). This is L0 hooks-only and it is a policy decision for security whether these harnesses may touch restricted repositories at all (SAF-008 class, TRU-001 unknown-identity policy).

### 4.4 What a team gets

Configuration artefacts, all versioned and most signed:

- `endpoint-inventory-v1.json` plus signature: deployments with rung, provider, api_base, trust zone, geo, data-use profile (TRU-002, OPS-003).
- `repo-classification-v1.json` plus signature: repository classes, residency, restricted prefixes, unknown-identity policy (SAF-008, TRU-001).
- `rungs.yaml`, `policy.yaml`, `prices.yaml`, `provider-pairs.yaml`, `tripwires.yaml`, `detectors.yaml`, `trusted-tool-servers.yaml`, `learning-contract-v1.json`, `utility-fingerprints.yaml`: every "versioned policy constant" in the register is a field here and is recorded on decision rows.
- A generated LiteLLM configuration with rung-closed fallback groups and local-only fallbacks for pinned aliases (`adrl gateway-config`).

Command line: `adrl serve`; `adrl config check` (eleven load-time checks including loopback-only local deployments, residency reachability, signed inventory and manifest, compaction feasibility, cross-rung fallback rejection); `adrl launch --repo`; `adrl gates audit --lineage`, `gates release`, `gates promote-shadow`, `gates scan-measure`; `adrl ledger replay`, `close`, `readiness`, `retention-sweep`, `erase`, `verify-begin`, `verify-finish`, `verify-egress`; `adrl cascade coverage`; `adrl learning build-dataset`, `train`, `evaluate`, `explore-check`; `adrl readiness`.

Telemetry: structured JSON logs with route and lineage context; Prometheus metrics for fail-open by class, gate latency, blocks, degraded memory and ledger, fingerprint divergence, shadow findings, pin write failures, hint contradictions, egress checkpoints and anchor age; GenAI semantic-convention attribute names so the gateway team and ADRL share vocabulary (Q7).

Audit answers: "did this lineage's content ever leave the machine, when, to which deployment in which trust zone and geo, under which verdicts, and is that confirmed by a receipt" (`adrl gates audit`); "who released which finding and why" (ledger); "is the egress ledger intact against the off-device anchor" (`adrl ledger verify-egress --public-key --anchors`).

Operator controls: per-subsystem modes (`gates enforce|observe`, `routing off|shadow|live`, `fallback off|shadow|live`); a one-step audited bypass by repointing the base URL, which cannot release a pin (FND-004); dev keys refused without an explicit flag (OPS-002).

## 5. Adoption path

**Week one, L0 shadow.** Repoint one team's Claude Code at ADRL with gates in observe mode and routing in shadow. Every request is classified and recorded, every decision the router would have made is logged, shadow findings show what the scanner would pin, and the egress ledger starts answering the audit question. Nothing changes for the developer. Run `adrl gates scan-measure` on the team's own labelled corpus to set detector tiers from measured precision (SAF-003).

**Move to L1, gates enforced.** Conditions from the register: the adversarial SAF suites have run and their results are published (SAF README ceiling); per-detector precision is published and the false-positive release fraction is measured over a shadow window (SAF-002, SAF-003); the residency and inventory checks pass for the team's repositories (TRU-002); the egress anchor is shipping and acknowledged (TRU-003, OPS-007). At L1 a pinned lineage stays local or blocks, so the team needs the local rung feasible at the 100k compaction floor (SAF-005) or accepts blocks.

**Move to L2, live routing for a constrained population.** FND-005 and EVL-007 govern this: every enabled rung carries an evidence reference (RTG-001), the local rung's permitted scope does not exceed task classes with a deterministic verifier (CAS-001, Q2), the ambiguous-band share and the baseline comparison have been measured on this population after cache effects (RTG-003, RTG-005, EVL-001), the excluded fraction is reported next to every metric (EVL-008), and no pre-registered blocker is open (EVL-009). Live routing refuses to load without evidence references, by construction.

**Roadmap items that block wider use.**

- SEM-007 Responses profile: without it Codex, Copilot BYOK on OpenAI formats, OpenCode's OpenAI-compatible provider and Junie's Responses profile stay at L0.
- Hook sidecar: Claude Code's `PostToolUseFailure` and MCP `is_error` events, and the hook surfaces of Copilot, Cline, Gemini CLI and Cursor, are not ingested yet (CAS-001 follow-up); L3 depends on it.
- Receipt contract with the gateway team: LiteLLM reports the served deployment on non-streamed responses but the critique found it omits the header on streamed ones, which is nearly all harness traffic; until the contract is agreed (Q7), served identity is `assumed_intended` on most inference and the audit reports it as unconfirmed.
- Verifier coverage: only task classes with a deterministic verifier can be local-scope candidates; the verification job and sandbox exist but the allow-listed command sets per repository do not.
- Multi-worker state (OPS-001): the shared team proxy is single-process until sticky and pin state move behind a multi-process provider.

## 6. Risks and open dispositions

The companion critique judged all 77 decisions against sources fetched on 2026-09-03. Its headline risks for this offering: full-transcript escalation recovers less than half the quality gap at a cost premium in a recent study, which pressures RTG-004 local-first and CAS-004's carry-the-transcript handoff; Anthropic's server-side compaction beta changes what a compaction request is (SEM-004); the harness sandbox SAF-007 relies on had four documented escapes in nine months; and the receipt mechanism has no vendor contract on streamed responses (OPS-006). None of these is fatal to the offering; each is a register amendment or a gateway-team agreement, and the critique lists the recommended change per decision.

Dispositions the register owner must make before the offering is described as more than a shadow deployment: accept or reject the TRU bucket and confirm SAF-008 and SAF-009 as superseded; dispose of the six proposed amendments (FND-002, RTG-001, RTG-007, CAS-003, MEM-005, MEM-010) and of SEM-007 and CAS-009; accept the EVL and OPS first captures or send them back. Until then, 22 of the 77 decisions are Proposed, and the offering's claims stop at what the Accepted decisions and the D1/D2 build support.
