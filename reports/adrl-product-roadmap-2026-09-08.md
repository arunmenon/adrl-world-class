## 1. The company we are trying to build

The customer should experience ADRL as a small addition to their existing coding workflow. A developer keeps their harness. The platform team defines which deployments may be used and what acceptable work means. ADRL chooses within those boundaries, observes progress, and produces an attributable record of the result.

The initial job to be done is:

> “Help my team finish eligible coding tasks at a lower total cost, without increasing defects or making developers manage model choices, and give me evidence I can defend.”

“Eligible” matters. A task without reliable verification, a harness that cannot expose the necessary state, or a repository whose policy prevents the proposed route may remain on the incumbent model or outside controlled routing. Report that excluded workload alongside any benefit.

The business outcome is not the percentage of requests sent to a cheap model. An inexpensive call that causes three failed attempts can be an expensive choice. Equally, paying more for a difficult step can be the economical decision if it avoids wasted work.

**Initial segment, as a hypothesis:** teams with roughly 50–300 engineers, an identifiable platform owner, an existing model API or gateway budget, and enough repeated, verifiable maintenance work to measure a difference. Organization size is a discovery filter, not a market estimate. Start with ordinary software businesses able to authorize a narrow evaluation; highly regulated deployments are a later segment unless an early partner brings a concrete, funded need.

| Person | What they need | What must convince them |
|---|---|---|
| Developer | Keep the familiar workflow; recover from a poor choice quickly | Working code, low friction, understandable decisions and an override within policy |
| Platform lead, initial champion | Control model access and support fewer one-off integrations | Reusable installation, compatibility evidence, predictable operation |
| VP Engineering or CTO, economic buyer | A worthwhile net benefit | Measured task outcomes, actual costs, adoption and support burden |
| Security/data owner | A clear, enforceable boundary | Tested data paths, retention, identity, recovery and documented blind spots |
| Finance or procurement | A reason to add a vendor | A budget owner, defensible attribution and a price below demonstrated value |

The first workflow should be bounded maintenance: small fixes, test repairs and modest refactors in repositories with known checks. Include difficult and mixed-intent cases so the router can learn when to spend more or abstain. Do not begin by promising autonomous delivery of arbitrary features.

Defer foundation-model training, a new coding IDE, a general agent platform, universal tool governance, seventeen production adapters, a model marketplace and a custom inference cloud. None is necessary to establish the initial business.

The older product report used the company engineering as its setting. That is historical project context, not a validated external customer, commercial reference or authorization to reuse internal material in sales.

### Our proposed advantage, and how it could fail

The hypothesis is a combination: **customer-controlled routing, outcomes tied to exact work, and reusable policies across qualified harnesses**. Each element has competitors. The combination becomes an advantage only if customers obtain a worthwhile result more easily than with alternatives.

Potential durability comes from reliable adapters, a trusted evaluation workflow, measured deployment/transition capability and a growing record of approved improvements. Algorithms and generic audit logs alone are easy to reproduce. Customer traces are not automatically a shared proprietary dataset: tenant boundaries, retention and explicit permissions constrain reuse.

We should be willing to integrate an existing selector if it wins. ADRL may own the permission, evidence and policy-release contracts while buying the routing scorer. If buyers value neither the incremental outcomes nor those controls enough to pay, the standalone startup thesis fails.

## 4. What the first product looks like

The first product has three visible experiences:

1. **A guided connection.** Choose the harness, approved deployments, repository and budget; run compatibility diagnostics; see exactly what ADRL can observe and control.
2. **A decision trail.** For each eligible decision, show task state, permitted choices, actual selected deployment, reason, policy version and receipt confidence. Later, show whether continuing or switching was justified.
3. **An improvement review.** Show the old policy, the evidence challenging it, the proposed change, independent comparison, disposition and rollback.

A leader should be able to follow one story:

> “This looked like a simple edit, but it also requested a concurrency redesign. The old rule chose the cheap route. The corrected rule recognizes the mixed scope. We then tested both choices on real tasks. Here is the quality, cost and repair difference. Here is the ADR changed as a result.”

That is the minimum narrative every engineering milestone must make more credible.

### Shared product contracts

The API should express six stable responsibilities: capability discovery; authenticated workload/session binding; decision and explanation; execution observations; exact completion/evidence; and policy lifecycle. These are responsibilities, not a claim that new endpoints already exist.

The SDK should hide routine transport work while preserving provider-specific state. Each adapter declares protocol/version, identity source, available signals, dispatch authority, transition boundaries, cancellation, unsupported features and tested deployments. Every event needs attributable versions and lineage; every mutable operation needs an idempotency/recovery contract.

A decision response must distinguish advice from an executed route. A dispatcher rechecks permission and deployment availability before sending. The evidence record distinguishes intended destination, provider-reported destination and unknown identity. A separate authorization surface controls policy activation.

Use customer-owned credentials and, initially, one customer-controlled installation with local/private evidence storage. A hosted administration service is optional later, with an explicit data contract. No compulsory cross-customer training or raw-code upload is part of the initial proposal. The product remains a single-tenant preview until operations and isolation earn broader promises.

### Local execution, offline learning, distributed knowledge

The user clarified the intended shape: **ADRL and experience memory on the developer laptop; qualified learning outside the live request path; improved knowledge distributed back to developers.** This fits the existing taxonomy. The [detailed architecture](../design/adrl-context-graph-memory-proposal-2026-09-08.md#the-laptop-and-offline-learning-architecture) distinguishes local observed experience from approved learned policy packages.

The harness supplies tool/work observations, while ADRL records its actual routing choices and reasons. MEM links them to outcomes. An offline learner, initially local or later organization-controlled, uses explicitly admitted evidence to propose a compact rule/table/estimator package. Independent evaluation and signing precede cohort rollout; each laptop checks compatibility and retains local restrictions and rollback. Its live routing need not depend on a central learner.

“Distillation” initially means shipping useful routing knowledge, without requiring coding-model training. Cross-developer evidence export and fleet distribution need explicit data, identity, compatibility, activation and revocation contracts. Cross-customer pooling is not assumed. This is a planned deployment application of MEM/LRN/EVL/OPS, not an implemented fleet service.

### Context is a first-class routing input

**User clarification incorporated, 8 September:** routing must consider the user request together with its context, and the plan must explain how the classifier and LRN contribute. This is central to the product, not a later optional enrichment.

“Fix this failing test” does not specify the required model capability. In one repository it means updating an expected string. In another, the failure reflects a race condition across several services. A model selector that sees only those five words cannot reliably distinguish the work. Context changes both the likelihood of completing it and the cost of each choice.

There are three kinds of context to distinguish:

- **Task context:** the relevant repository, files, constraints, acceptance criteria, tools and prior conversation. This determines what the task means.
- **Execution context:** what has happened so far, which model is active, available budget, current cache/context state and whether a legal switching boundary exists.
- **Evidence context:** what earlier, qualified outcomes tell us about these deployments on comparable work. This can improve the estimate, but retrieval retains MEM-008's authority gates.

The router does not necessarily need to read every token the coding model will see. It needs the information that changes the decision, within the authorized privacy, latency and cost budget. “Send the whole transcript to another large model before every call” can consume the benefit routing was meant to produce. Conversely, a summary that throws away the difficult part of the task can make an otherwise capable advisor useless.

| Context signal | How it can change the decision | Required treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Full task scope, including mixed instructions | A small requested edit may include a substantial redesign | Preserve multiple intents and uncertainty; do not reduce everything to the first matching verb |
| Relevant files, language and dependencies | Similar wording may involve a single function or a distributed change | Use authorized, task-relevant signals; large repository size alone is not difficulty |
| Acceptance criteria and available verification | Some work has clear tests; other work needs human/domain review | Missing verification changes eligibility and confidence; it is not proof of difficulty or success |
| Available tools and side effects | A capable model may still be incompatible with the harness or unsafe to switch | Treat feasibility and permission as restrictions, not merely score penalties |
| Context completeness and freshness | Missing files or stale summaries may explain uncertainty better than weak model capability | Record unknown/missing/stale separately; a more expensive model cannot manufacture missing facts |
| Current model, cache, context fit and budget | Switching may lose useful state or cost more than continuing | Use deployment-specific costs and semantic compatibility |
| Earlier progress and failures | A task that initially looked easy may now warrant reconsideration | Use only reliably attributed information already available at the current boundary |
| Comparable verified past outcomes | A model may have a measured advantage for a particular slice | Respect provenance, time cutoffs, privacy and learning/retrieval admission |

Context is also a constraint on the answer. A model with a better average score may be the wrong choice if it cannot accept the available state, use the tools correctly, or operate inside the permitted environment. A private task may rule out the nominally strongest model altogether.

**A classifier and LRN have different jobs.** The classifier compresses information into a useful description or advisory category. LRN-003 is explicitly designed to estimate the additional verified benefit of one model choice over another, given decision-time features, with uncertainty. It does not simply emit a model name. RTG combines that estimate with costs and constraints to select an action.

| Component | Its question | Taxonomy |
|---|---|---|
| Request/intent and scope classification | What is this request asking us to do? | SEM; RTG-003; decision-time features in LRN-004 |
| Bounded model advisor | In an ambiguous permitted case, what route does the advisor recommend? | RTG-006 |
| Learned comparative estimator | In this context, how much better is one qualified deployment likely to perform than another? | LRN-003 with LRN-004/006; runtime target RTG-007 |
| Decision policy | Is that expected benefit worth its cost, and is the action permitted now? | RTG-002/005/009; TRU/SAF; CAS for transitions |

The decision chain is:

**Request + available context → attributed, permitted state snapshot → rules/advisor or qualified LRN estimate → cost and constraint decision → actual dispatch → verified outcome.**

The outcome enters the next training/review cycle only when admitted. At a later safe boundary, already-observed state can support a new decision. The final result of that new decision is never available as its input.

**Current implementation is only a partial version of this design.** The feature extractor records context length estimates, message/tool counts, verb/scope hints, tool-risk information and coarse recent-error signals. Its embedding field explicitly says not computed. The optional RTG advisor receives a small derived feature subset, not the full request or repository context. The runtime router constructs the heuristic estimator; the separate LRN estimator implementation is not wired into that selector. This was checked against [features](../../adrl-core/src/adrl/routing/features.py), [advisor](../../adrl-core/src/adrl/routing/advisor.py), [router](../../adrl-core/src/adrl/routing/router.py) and [LRN estimator](../../adrl-core/src/adrl/learning/estimator.py). The prior diagnostic configured no advisor.

That exposes an important failure mode: if feature extraction reduces “rename this variable and redesign concurrency” to “small mechanical edit,” adding a stronger classifier downstream may not recover the discarded requirement. Correcting the state representation is therefore part of P1, before investing in a more sophisticated learner.

The proposed context contract should carry a version, decision/boundary ID, authorized source references, observation time, freshness/missingness and uncertainty. Fields include task scope, relevant context summaries, tool/verification availability, approved prior-step signals, current deployment/context/cache state and remaining budget. The specific field list must be disposed and tested; these are proposed responsibilities, not an implemented schema.

Prefer deterministic, low-cost signals first. Test a bounded local semantic representation only if it adds value. Use a costed advisor where already allowed, and use qualified historical evidence once its gates pass. Derived summaries and embeddings may themselves reveal sensitive information: their extraction, transmission and storage require the same explicit data-boundary treatment.

The router initially consumes context the authorized harness/profile supplies. Building a repository indexer, autonomously retrieving more code, changing compaction, or asking the harness to acquire context is a separate action and protocol contract. Do not quietly expand the router into a second coding agent. If extra context could help, assess its expected decision benefit against acquisition cost and exposure; proceed only within a later approved capability.

**Context must earn its place in an experiment.** P2/P3 should compare nested candidates: request features alone; request plus permitted task context; then task context plus pre-boundary trajectory; and later qualified historical evidence. Hold the allowed deployments, evaluator, task assignment and budget basis constant. Include feature/advisor overhead. Use fresh same-request/different-context cases, plus irrelevant-context and stale/missing-context checks. Routing should change when a meaningful contextual difference changes expected benefit, not merely because a prompt became longer.

Illustrative cases, not measured outcomes:

| Same request | Context A | Context B | Hypothesis to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Fix this failing test” | One stale expected value with clear checks | Concurrency failure after repeated unsuccessful edits | B may justify a different initial route or qualified escalation |
| “Explain this function” | Short pure function | Function depends on missing dynamic configuration | B should expose uncertainty; upgrading alone may not solve missing context |
| “Complete this refactor” | Existing model is making verified progress with warm context | Current model is looping and a compatible switch is available | Continue may win in A; switching may win in B |
| “Update this parser” | Similar verified tasks support a model choice | Unfamiliar language/domain outside training support | B should increase abstention, even if the task label matches |

One ADR tension needs explicit resolution: LRN-004 currently has broad prohibitions on verification and escalation features. The temporal design wants to use trustworthy results of *earlier* steps. Define a time-indexed allowlist for past observations at a new boundary while continuing to prohibit the current action's outcome and future information. Until that amendment is disposed, those new learned inputs are not authorized. Persist the exact snapshot used so training cannot reconstruct it later with hindsight.

This makes LRN a planned consumer of meaningful context from the beginning. Training and live learned authority still arrive only after their existing evidence gates; context-aware deterministic routing can be demonstrated much earlier.

### Connected decision memory in MEM

The user's subsequent clarification is specifically a **context graph for memory and adaptation**. The [context-graph proposal](../design/adrl-context-graph-memory-proposal-2026-09-08.md) makes that explicit: task/context → decision and allowed alternatives → actual attempt → verified or corrected outcome → evaluated policy/ADR change.

The proposal begins with a complete MEM-001–010 crosswalk and current source inspection. MEM already supplies the architectural foundation for remembering, correcting and retrieving experience. NumpyIndex, ShadowRetriever, historical-neighbour feature machinery and the offline experiment archive exist. Their operational qualification and contribution to model selection remain the work to prove. A new graph is not a prerequisite for benefiting from existing MEM.

Place this as a proposed MEM-007 projection with MEM-008 retrieval gates, drawing on MEM's existing event, verification, closure and counterfactual identity contracts. Repository relationships are one supporting context source. Decision experience and improvement history are the core memory.

The early question is whether those connections improve relevant evidence retrieval and subsequent routing. Test structured fields, flat/vector retrieval and bounded graph retrieval on the same authorized evidence. Include indexing/refresh costs, missing and contradictory evidence, privacy filtering and historical time cutoffs. A retrieved relationship does not establish the causal effect of a model choice.

P1 audits and demonstrates existing links/retrieval, identifies missing context and defines any candidate graph additions; P2 establishes trustworthy real experience; P3 tests the usefulness of existing retrieval and bounded enrichment in shadow; P5 admits qualified memory-derived features/evidence for policy learning. P6/P7 can use the history of accepted and rejected experiments to improve the improvement process. No new graph database, broad repository index or graph-based live authority is approved by this plan.

## 6. The long-horizon roadmap

Time ranges below are capacity assumptions from an explicitly authorized restart, not delivery commitments. They assume a CTO, two product/runtime engineers and one evaluation engineer, with product ownership and independent review available. The present single-agent workflow does not constitute that staffed team. Partner access, evidence volume and release gates can move dates.

| Milestone | Indicative horizon | Product proof | Existing engineering work | Investment unlocked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0. Validate the reason to exist | Weeks 1–2 | A buyer has a measurable problem that alternatives do not adequately solve | W0 baseline and architecture dispositions | Small product experiment |
| P1. Make routing visible and dependable | Weeks 2–4 | Real selector, actual controlled test dispatch, faithful explanations | W7.0 correction; minimum W3/W4/W6 prerequisites | Fund real task comparisons |
| P2. Prove value on one harness | Weeks 4–8, extend for evidence | Better complete-task economics at acceptable quality on a declared scope | W7 comparisons, relevant W3/W4/W6 | Restricted customer pilot, only after applicable graduation |
| P3. Prove adaptation and a repeatable pilot | Weeks 8–12+, evidence-dependent | Task-state changes improve decisions; a customer chooses to continue | CAS/RTG work, W7 and a small W9 pre-build assessment | Broader integration and productization |
| P4. Make the product reusable | Months 4–6 | Second harness plus admitted Responses path; repeatable installation | W5, W8A, W8B | Stable supported API on qualified scope |
| P5. Learn better routing policies | Months 6–9, only if upside/data gates pass | A new policy beats the incumbent on fresh evidence | W9; MEM/LRN/EVL | Bounded proposal automation |
| P6. Reduce the cost of improving ADRL | Months 9–12 | Automated proposals produce useful, independently approved changes | W10; selected W11 needs | A controlled RSI experiment |
| P7. Test RSI and selective scale | Months 12–18 | A better improver produces more durable net benefit for the same budget | W12; demand-led W11 | Further research/scale only if both technical and commercial evidence support it |

Customer discovery and distribution work run alongside engineering throughout. P4 can overlap with P3 after the first useful pilot evidence; it must not consume the capacity needed to settle whether routing works. P5 research can analyze eligible data earlier, but building a learned estimator retains RTG-007's pre-build gate.

### P0: Validate the reason to exist

**Owner:** product lead and CTO. **Entry:** this draft and current evidence inventory.

Interview a proposed 12–15 participants across at least eight organizations, including platform owners, developers and economic buyers. Seek permission to inspect actual cost reports, recent failed/repaired tasks and present model-selection workflows. Record the current alternative and why the buyer has not solved the problem with it.

Discovery questions: Which work is expensive? How much spending can actually move? Who handles failures? Which data must stay inside the organization? Which harnesses are mandatory? Who can approve a trial and a purchase? What would cause a switch back?

**Proposed exit:** three organizations willing to define a trial, at least two with a named budget owner and feasible access, and a measurable pain statement supported by artifacts. These are discovery targets, not statistically representative research or customer commitments already obtained. Prepare a comparison against LiteLLM/native routing; pursue Not Diamond access only with separate authorization.

**Stop/replan:** no controllable budget, no testable workflow, or existing alternatives solve the problem at an acceptable cost. Revisit the segment before funding broader engineering. Resolve the P1/P2 blocking ADR questions in section 10.

### P1: Make routing visible and dependable

**Owner:** runtime lead with evaluation review. **Entry:** approved scoped packet, frozen diagnostic baseline and declared conformance profile.

Correct mixed-intent/wording defects without replacing the entire router. Specify and inspect the minimum decision-time context snapshot; preserve the difficult part of mixed tasks before any advisor sees it. Add explanations from the actual decision path. Demonstrate initial choice, allowed continuation, boundary-limited escalation, abstention, policy denial and a typed unavailable state. Show a real dispatch receipt from controlled endpoints; mark synthetic endpoints plainly.

**Exit demonstration:** replay the preserved failing cases, run fresh paraphrases and mixed tasks, and show the selected destination matching the actual controlled dispatch. Exercise streaming, cancellation, side-effect boundaries and restriction rechecks for the named profile. No outcome or savings claim comes from these fixtures.

Complete only the custody, exact-output verification and model-path controls necessary for the next real experiment. Active-copy erasure, exact-close association and a concrete real-harness profile remain blockers for real payload capture.

Include a minimal decision-memory graph design and one synthetic traversal under MEM-007/008. This defines the evidence connections without introducing a broad memory-platform dependency.

**Stop/replan:** explanations do not match execution, a restriction can be bypassed, or accounting loses attempts. Broader packaging waits. This milestone strengthens scoped D2 evidence; it does not create D3/D4.

### P2: Prove value on one harness

**Owner:** evaluation lead; CTO accountable for execution; product lead owns value assessment. **Entry:** qualified capture/verifier/dispatch, approved access and finite budget, resolved applicable evaluation contract.

Begin with Claude-family routes on Claude Code, existing approved rules and fixed-model baselines. Use owned or explicitly authorized repositories and independent checks. Include at least three task families and two repositories in the diagnostic set. Thirty development tasks are a starting debugging budget, not efficacy proof.

Run independently executed alternatives from the same valid initial state. Freeze model/profile versions and measure full attempts, including failures, retries, cache, verification and repair. Use separate later confirmation tasks. Research comparisons remain separately labelled until evidence admission is explicitly satisfied.

Include the context ablations in section 4: determine whether permitted task context improves decisions over request-only features, then test the additional value of pre-boundary trajectory in P3. A changed model choice is a mechanism observation; better completed-task results establish whether that context helped.

**Exit:** an auditable comparison reports acceptable quality, total cost and uncertainty; the buyer sees worthwhile net value at an indicated price. Formal exposure still requires EVL's applicable gates and review. If confidence is insufficient, the result is insufficient, even if the point estimate is favorable.

**Stop/replan:** quality loss, negative net value, untrustworthy verification, insufficient controllable spend or no advantage over a practical alternative. Try one bounded diagnosis-driven correction; do not launch an indefinite algorithm search. A failed economic hypothesis is a legitimate result.

### P3: Prove adaptation and a repeatable pilot

**Owner:** runtime/evaluation leads with the customer champion. **Entry:** P2 evidence and explicitly approved bounded exposure.

Compare an initial-choice-only policy with one that uses qualified progress/failure signals at safe boundaries. Measure whether switching helps after paying handoff and cache costs. A higher model-use count is not success. Add rule-health refresh only with specified admissible sources, freshness, observation thresholds and rollback behavior.

**Visible example:** a permitted lower-cost model starts a test repair; a verified failure changes the state; ADRL either continues with justification or follows an approved transition. The comparison shows whether that intervention improves completed tasks.

**Proposed commercial exit:** two design partners complete the agreed evaluation; at least one pays to continue, and an additional budget owner confirms a purchase path. Record installation help, developer opt-outs, manual overrides, eligible coverage and weekly use. These are early repeatability signals, not product-market fit.

For the small operational pilot, retain the prior proposed floor of two weeks and 100 eligible task attempts, plus enough cases to close the actual gates. Duration and volume do not substitute for efficacy evidence. No customer release bypasses unresolved safety or graduation requirements.

**Stop/replan:** adaptation adds cost without benefit, developers repeatedly disable it, or each partner needs bespoke engineering. Retain the simpler winning selector and narrow the scope.

### P4: Make the product reusable

**Owner:** integrations lead. **Entry:** useful first-harness evidence and a second partner/workflow worth serving.

Qualify OpenCode against the same decision/evidence contracts. Exercise the local/provider options independently; each transition earns its own support entry. Admit Responses under SEM-007 and qualify the planned Codex path. Keep unsupported features explicit.

**Exit:** the FND-005 two-harness/two-protocol requirement is actually met; a fresh integrator can follow the SDK/profile guide; compatibility, migration, cancellation, identity, state continuity, retention and rollback are demonstrated. Proposed setup target: a diagnostic within 15 minutes once credentials/models/policy are ready, with assistance and failures included.

A stable API is a compatibility commitment. Publish versioned schemas, deprecation policy, conformance fixtures, support matrix and release notes only for qualified behavior. A second adapter file is insufficient.

**Stop/replan:** protocol transformations silently lose meaning or integration remains bespoke. Keep preview status and narrow supported features; do not solve this by claiming every harness is equivalent.

### P5: Learn better routing policies

**Owner:** evaluation/learning lead; independent release authority. **Entry:** RTG-007 upside assessment and EVL-004 data/advisory gates, reconciled T1/T5 rules, tested abstention, and a qualified incumbent.

Start with a measured capability table or small comparative estimator. Compare it with the current heuristic and strong fixed policies. Train only on admitted evidence; preserve provenance, privacy exclusions and fresh temporal/session holdouts. Measure calibration and coverage as well as average benefit.

**Exit:** a candidate produces worthwhile independently confirmed routing improvement, passes the complete evaluation report and receives scoped promotion. Show one trace from a changed decision through its verified result and owning ADR.

**Stop/replan:** little ambiguous-band spend, small achievable upside, insufficient diverse evidence, unstable calibration or gains consumed by inference/evaluation overhead. Keep rules or a bought selector. A startup can have an adaptive product without a proprietary learned router.

### P6: Reduce the cost of improving ADRL

**Owner:** CTO and evaluation lead. **Entry:** several manually completed, independently reviewed improvement cycles with useful outcomes.

Automate failure grouping, candidate preparation and experiment packets within a fixed search space. Keep proposal artifacts separate from deployable authority. Every proposal records its data, tools, cost, rejected variants, ADR impact and rollback.

**Exit:** over multiple cycles, proposal automation reduces the total effort needed to obtain durable approved improvements. Include unsuccessful proposals and reviewer time. Run later confirmation so a clever patch cannot win only on known examples.

**Stop/replan:** review overhead erases the gain, proposal quality degrades, or evaluator/data access is breached. Return to the manual process. This is improvement automation; it is not yet a demonstrated recursive gain.

### P7: Test RSI and selective scale

**Owner:** CTO; independent evaluator and release signer. **Entry:** P6 is useful and repeatable, plus a customer reason to invest further.

Compare improver A with improver B from the same incumbent, equivalent permitted evidence and the same total budget. B may change how failures are grouped, experiments selected or proposals constructed. Compare the quality of resulting routing policies on fresh tasks, then confirm durability.

**Exit:** B produces greater independently accepted net routing benefit per total search, evaluation and review cost across multiple task families and later windows. Preserve failed searches and predeclare stopping/selection rules. One successful self-edit does not establish RSI.

Scale tenant management, administration, support and distribution only where paid demand justifies them. Single-process assumptions cannot be advertised as multi-worker safety; tenant isolation, key ownership and recovery need their own qualification.

**Stop/replan:** no reproducible improver advantage, no commercial return, or the improver needs to relax its own judge/permissions to win. The underlying routing product can continue without RSI.

## 8. Measurement that can survive scrutiny

The primary product outcome is **verified acceptable completion at lower total cost on the eligible workload**. Report completion rate, cost per accepted task and repair burden together; any one alone can be misleading.

Count every assigned attempt, including abandoned, failed and indeterminate work. Cost per accepted task includes the cost of unsuccessful assigned attempts. If there are no accepted tasks, it is undefined, not zero. Keep unknown verification separate from failure, and publish a conservative sensitivity analysis rather than dropping it.

| Measurement | What is included |
|---|---|
| Task quality | Independent acceptance, false acceptance/rejection, later repair/revert within a declared window |
| Cash cost | Billed model usage, cache writes/reads, retries, advisor calls, tools, attributable local compute and routine verification |
| Time | Task elapsed time, developer/reviewer active effort, waiting and intervention; avoid double-counting concurrent agent time |
| Routing quality | Initial placement, continuation/switch benefit, unnecessary switching, abstention coverage, missed failures |
| Controls | Forbidden dispatch, unconfirmed identity, incomplete accounting, protocol loss, recovery/retention violations |
| Product adoption | Eligible/total workload, active pilot developers, opt-outs, overrides, onboarding and support effort |
| Improvement efficiency | Durable accepted benefit divided by all proposal/search/evaluation/reviewer costs |

Separate steady-state task costs from one-off research/evaluation spending, then show the latter's amortization. Separate cash savings from an assumption-based valuation of developer time. A fixed subscription does not create marginal cash savings merely because fewer tokens are used.

**Experiment protocol:** freeze task eligibility, baseline versions, model deployments, price basis, quality endpoint, acceptable loss margin, worthwhile gain, confidence method, holdout minimum, budget and exclusions before evaluating a release candidate. Use task/session as the appropriate assignment unit, account for developer/repository clustering, and keep related variants in one split. Do not treat repeat runs as new independent tasks.

Use a practical incumbent chosen before the holdout, plus the register's required comparators. The hindsight-best single model is an analytical bound, not a deployable selector with advance knowledge. Native or commercial routing is an additional comparison when accessible under equivalent constraints; absent access means untested, not defeated.

For early research, 30 development tasks diagnose problems. For learned authority, EVL-004 retains its working minimum of 300 qualifying labels plus quality, diversity and suppression gates. Neither number proves statistical power. A reviewer must size the confirmation window for the actual quality margin and expected gain; inconclusive results do not pass.

**Proposed commercial hurdle:** test whether a customer would pay when confirmed net value is at least three times the subscription fee, with no unacceptable quality/repair regression. This is a pricing/discovery hypothesis, not an ADR graduation threshold. Minimum routing gain and quality tolerances remain separately versioned and disposed under RTG/EVL.

Operational starting targets carried from the existing roadmap are: complete attempt accounting; no critical violation in the named suite; stop new affected work within 60 seconds of an exercised kill switch; restore a compatible approved service within ten minutes where safe. These are targets to verify before promising them, not present SLAs.

## 9. RSI, explained through the product

There are three distinct improvements:

| Loop | Example | What changes | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|---|
| During the task | A trustworthy failed check makes continuing less attractive | A decision inside the approved policy | Safe boundary, valid state and a better completed-task result |
| Across tasks | Verified parser fixes show the starting rule is unreliable | A versioned routing policy | Admitted outcomes, independent comparison and scoped promotion |
| Recursive improvement | A better failure-analysis method selects more useful experiments | The process that proposes policy improvements | A/B comparison of improvers, equal budgets, fresh judging and durable gains |

RSI helps only if the third loop makes the second loop more productive. It may recommend a taxonomy change: for example, evidence could challenge a local-first assumption or justify a narrowly defined downshift experiment. It does not grant itself authority to amend that decision.

Mutable candidates include rule thresholds, representations, estimators, calibration and proposal strategies within approved bounds. Permission rules, data access, budget ceilings, protected evaluation standards and release authority remain outside the candidate's control. An improved verifier is evaluated separately from the policy it judges.

A rule correction and a rewritten report are useful course corrections. They are not proof of RSI. The early product sells a useful routing outcome; RSI is a later way to improve its economics and quality.

### Maturity is earned, not scheduled

D0 means specified; D1 implemented; D2 tested for a named scope; D3 observed on qualified organic shadow traffic; D4 approved for a restricted pilot; D5 graduated for continued supported use in that population. Each promotion requires the existing EVL-007 evidence and recorded authority, one rung at a time.

P1 can strengthen D2 without any commercial improvement. P2/P3 may earn scoped D3/D4 only after the applicable conflicts and gates are resolved. P4 may qualify more profiles without improving routing quality. P5 may leave one candidate at D2 while another approved policy serves the pilot. P7 may fail and leave RSI as research.

Do not average ordinal maturity levels into an ADRL percentage. The leader view should show: customer milestone, currently supported scope, blocking ADR clauses, evidence added, reviewer disposition and next investment gate. Model/harness changes or regressions can invalidate evidence and narrow support.

## 11. Business model, resources and investment

### Packaging and pricing hypothesis

Offer a time-bounded paid design-partner evaluation with explicit deliverables and no savings guarantee. Customer model/provider charges remain separate. Successful customers move to an annual platform subscription covering a defined active-developer or usage band, administration, evidence reports and support.

Test pricing before building a sophisticated billing system. An initial conversation anchor could be $1,000–$3,000 per team per month where the eligible spend and operational benefit support it. These are unvalidated price hypotheses, not market prices or a proposed charge to this user. Larger private deployments need a separately costed support contract.

Avoid a pure token markup that rewards more consumption. Shared-savings pricing is premature while causal attribution and cash-versus-time benefits remain uncertain.

### Illustrative customer economics

For cash savings only:

**Monthly net benefit = addressable model spend × eligible coverage × measured reduction − incremental operating/evaluation cost − ADRL fee.**

The following uses invented planning inputs, not observations: $40,000 monthly model spend, $1,000 incremental monthly cost and a $1,500 ADRL fee. Quality must first meet the agreed requirement.

| Scenario | Eligible coverage | Measured reduction assumed | Gross saving | Net after $2,500 costs/fee |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 25% | 10% | $1,000 | −$1,500 |
| Middle | 50% | 20% | $4,000 | $1,500 |
| High | 75% | 30% | $9,000 | $6,500 |

At 50% coverage and a 20% reduction, cash break-even requires $25,000 monthly addressable model spend under these assumptions. The middle case clears break-even but fails the proposed three-times-fee value hurdle. This is why coverage, eligible spend and a buyer's actual alternatives matter more than a headline routing percentage.

Do not add a monetary productivity benefit unless the customer accepts the measured effort change and valuation assumptions. Do not count one-off evaluation expense twice between this illustration and the pilot budget.

### Founding team and capacity

| Role | Initial responsibility |
|---|---|
| CTO | Architecture, scope, technical risk and build-versus-buy decisions |
| Runtime/integration engineer | Dispatch, semantics, adapters and recovery |
| Product/SDK engineer | Setup, decision trail, APIs and operability |
| Evaluation/learning engineer | Task evidence, experiments, quality and later policy learning |
| Product lead/founder | Discovery, pricing, partner commitments and investment decisions |
| Independent part-time reviewers | Evaluation challenge, security scope and release decisions outside the candidate-producing team |

Start with that small team model. Add customer engineering/support when partner onboarding consumes material engineering capacity; add dedicated ML research only after the upside/data gate. Automated coding can assist implementation, but it does not replace an independent evaluator or a customer owner.

**Illustrative first-90-day funding envelope:** four technical people × three months × an assumed $15,000 fully loaded monthly cost = $180,000; evaluation/compute $15,000; independent review $15,000; operations/legal/tools $15,000; 20% contingency on $225,000 = $45,000; total $270,000. Product-founder compensation is excluded and must be added if salaried. These are budgeting assumptions, not compensation benchmarks, quotations or authorized spending. Reprice for geography, existing staff and partner contribution.

Release funding in tranches: P0 discovery and trial specification; P1/P2 engineering and measured comparison; P3 paid continuation; P4 repeatable product; P5–P7 only after evidence of incremental return. Do not commit an 18-month budget to an unvalidated routing algorithm.

### Distribution and repeatability

Use founder-led discovery and design partners first. The practical entry point is a platform team's existing harness/gateway workflow, not an organization-wide replacement program. Prepare a technical trial guide, explicit capability matrix, decision walkthrough and evidence-backed result.

After repeatability, distribute an integration SDK, public conformance examples and reproducible non-sensitive benchmarks, subject to ownership/licensing review. Evaluate partnerships with gateways or harness vendors as a route to customers. A public adapter can reduce adoption friction; a paid control/evidence service must have independently valued functionality.

Track the funnel from qualified conversation to trial readiness, activation, completed evaluation, paid continuation and renewal. Track time and engineering effort at every step. Do not hire a broad sales team before a second customer can adopt substantially the same product.

There is no defensible TAM estimate yet. Build a bottom-up market model from reachable organizations that have sufficient eligible spend, technical access, urgency and budget ownership, multiplied by tested willingness to pay. Survey-wide AI adoption is not our serviceable market.

## 12. Risks, guardrails and course correction

| Risk | Early signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Routing is becoming bundled infrastructure | Buyers prefer native/commodity alternatives | Benchmark them, integrate where useful, stop the standalone thesis if no paid advantage remains |
| Savings disappear after retries/cache/repair | Cheap calls, expensive completed tasks | Optimize total task benefit; retain stronger fixed route where it wins |
| Incorrect labels train worse policies | Passed checks disagree with independent review | Quarantine labels, repair measurement separately, obtain fresh evaluation |
| Broader context creates privacy pressure | Useful signals require disallowed content | Reduce features or scope; never quietly expand data collection |
| Models/harnesses change faster than qualification | Previously supported transitions regress | Pin/version profiles, run change qualification, withdraw affected support |
| Infrastructure consumes the roadmap | Work has no demonstrable effect on the next routing experiment | Require a named blocked product gate and bounded exit before scheduling it |
| Small pilots exaggerate success | One developer/repository dominates results | Report coverage and uncertainty; recruit independent scopes before expansion |
| Enterprise features overwhelm a startup | Every pilot needs custom tenancy or deployment | Start private single-tenant, price support, add shared scale only with demand |
| RSI optimizes its own score | Judge changes, reused holdouts, hidden search cost | Separate permissions and evaluation; reject contaminated evidence |

Any forbidden dispatch, forged authority, cross-session data leak, duplicate external effect or erasure resurrection stops the affected controlled scope. Preserve evidence and restrictions during rollback. Zero violations in a test suite is not a guarantee about every future request.

For each failed gate, record one of four outcomes: continue within the predeclared experiment budget; narrow the claim or population; change the hypothesis through ADR disposition; or stop the investment. Do not revise a success threshold after looking at results merely to declare a pass.

## 13. Operating cadence and the decision now

After implementation is authorized, run a weekly product/evidence review and a fortnightly visible routing demonstration.
